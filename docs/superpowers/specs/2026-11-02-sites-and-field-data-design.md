# Customer groups, sites and field data

- **Date:** 2026-11-02
- **Status:** draft for review (owner answers from 2026-11-02, two rounds, included; open questions at the end)
- **Amends:** ADR-0004 (organizations get one level of children) and ADR-0006 (a new `worker` role; partners and workers see the sites of their own projects). An ADR-0008 records this design once it is approved, and an ADR-0009 records the offline approach (ADR-0007 is reserved for time zones).

## Intent
A customer is often a large organization with sub-brands or sub-companies, and the work happens at physical sites. Both the organization and each sub-company have their own contacts and general information. Every project is work at one site. Technicians and electrical staff go to the site and record what they find: visit notes, photos, readings on forms that admins define, and reports, both uploaded and generated by the app. They are internal **workers** or partner users. Sites often have no signal, so field capture has to work offline and sync later **without losing data**.

## Owner decisions (2026-11-02)
1. **One level of hierarchy:** organization → sub-companies (sub-brands). A sub-company has nothing under it.
2. **Sites are separate from the hierarchy.** A site is a physical location and belongs to an organization or to a sub-company.
3. **Every project has exactly one site.** New projects require one.
4. **Field data:** visits with free-form notes, photos, structured readings and report files.
5. **Partners are assigned per organization.** Assigning a parent gives access to the parent's own projects only, not to its sub-companies.
6. **Technicians are internal workers and partner users.** Internal technicians get their own role, `worker`.
7. **Partner users and workers see the site details and on-site contacts** of their own projects' sites. For the customer or sub-company they still see only the name.
8. **Only admins create and change sites** (may change later).
9. **Admins build form templates** for readings in the app.
10. **Reports:** both uploaded files and reports generated by the app.
11. **Offline:** a technician can open a project's forms with no signal, fill them in, take photos and record the visit. Everything syncs when service returns, and nothing recorded on the device may be lost.

## Roles (amends ADR-0006)
A new membership role, `worker` (shown as "Worker"), for internal technicians. It has no partner. Rules marked *my call* are for review.

| | Admin | Auditor | Worker | Partner user |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Projects they see | all | all (read) | projects whose **crew** they're on | own partner's projects |
| Project settings, cancel, copy, delete | yes | no | no (*my call*) | as today (ADR-0006) |
| Create projects | yes | no | no (*my call*) | as today |
| Tasks in their projects | full | read | add, edit, change status; no delete (*my call*) | full |
| Visits, photos, readings, reports in their projects | full | read | record and upload; delete only their own (*my call*) | record and upload; delete only their own (*my call*) |
| Site details and on-site contacts | all sites | all (read) | sites of their projects | sites of their projects |
| Customers | full | read | **name only** | **name only** (assigned customers) |
| Sites: create, edit, delete | yes | no | no | no |
| Form templates | build, publish, archive | read | fill published ones | fill published ones |

- **Crew:** `project_worker` (`project_id`, `user_id`). Admins add workers to a project's crew from the project page (*my call*). A worker can be on any project in the workspace, internal or a partner's. Removing a worker from a crew clears their task assignments in that project, the same way task 052 handles demoted auditors.
- Workers can be assigned tasks in their crew's projects.
- A new access function, `can_work_on_project`, covers recording field data and changing tasks. It is true for admins, for a partner on their own projects, and for workers on their crew's projects. `can_change_project` (settings, cancel, copy, delete) stays as it is, and is false for workers.

## Data model

### Organization hierarchy (amends ADR-0004)
- `organization.parent_id`: nullable, a reference to `organization`, in the same workspace and of the same kind. A row that has a parent can't be a parent itself (one level). Code checks this, and a trigger enforces it.
- A sub-company is a full organization row with its own status, contact details, general info, tags, contacts, notes and primary contact. It reuses every customer component.
- A project's or task's `organization_id` may point to a parent or to a sub-company.
- Soft delete (*my call*): deleting a parent is refused while it has sub-companies that aren't deleted ("Delete its sub-companies first"). Restoring a sub-company whose parent is deleted gets "Restore its parent first".

### Sites
- `site`: `id`, `workspace_id`, `organization_id` (a parent or a sub-company), `name`, `address`, `access_notes` (gate codes, opening hours, safety notes), optional `latitude`/`longitude`, `tags`, timestamps, soft-delete columns.
- `site_contact`: `site_id`, `name`, `role`, `phone`, `email`, plus soft-delete columns. These are on-site people, separate from the organization's contacts.
- `project.site_id`: the site must belong to the project's organization or to one of its sub-companies. Code checks this, and so does a trigger.
  - **Required** in the new-project and edit-project forms, and when copying a project, where the copy keeps the site.
  - The column stays nullable in the database, because existing projects have no site. Their project page shows "Add a site", and saving their settings asks for one.
  - The demo seed and the e2e fixtures create sites.
- **Choosing a site:** a partner creating a project must pick a site, but can't see sites yet. A `site_choice` view (like `customer_name`) returns `id`, `name` and `organization_id` of sites belonging to the organizations assigned to the partner. Full details stay behind `can_see_site`.

### Field data
All field records have a **client-generated id** (UUID) so a retried sync can't create duplicates, and soft-delete columns.
- `site_visit`: `id`, `project_id`, `visited_on` (a calendar `date`), `notes`, `created_by`, `created_at`, `recorded_at` (when it happened on the device). `site_visit_member` lists the people who were there. The visit's site is the project's site.
- `attachment`: `id`, `workspace_id`, `project_id`, nullable `visit_id`, `kind` (`photo | file | report`), `storage_key`, `file_name`, `content_type`, `size_bytes`, `caption`, `uploaded_by`, `created_at`, `recorded_at`. The bytes go to object storage; only metadata lives in Postgres.
- **Form templates (readings):**
  - `form_template`: `workspace_id`, `name`, `description`, `status` (`draft | published | archived`).
  - `form_template_version`: `template_id`, `version`, `published_at`. Immutable once published, so readings always match the fields they were filled against, including readings filled in offline against a version that has since been replaced.
  - `form_field`: `version_id`, `key`, `label`, `type`, `unit`, `required`, `options`, `min`/`max`, `position`. The field types are a `const` list; the exact set waits on open question 2.
- `form_submission`: `id`, `visit_id`, `version_id`, `values` (`jsonb`, checked against the version's fields by a parser in code), `submitted_by`, `recorded_at`, `submitted_at` (*my call*: `jsonb` rather than one row per value).
  - *My call:* a technician picks any published template. Required forms per project are in the parking lot.
- `generated_report`: `project_id`, the visits it covers, `generated_by`, `generated_at`, `storage_key` of the PDF. A report is a snapshot; regenerating creates a new one.

### Access (amends ADR-0006)
- New SQL access functions in the existing style (`SECURITY DEFINER`, `search_path = public, pg_temp`, `EXECUTE` for `app_user` only), with row-level security policies on every new table:
  - `access_role` gains `worker`, and `can_see_project` includes crew membership.
  - `can_work_on_project` (see Roles).
  - `can_see_site`: admins and auditors; partners and workers only when they can see a project at that site.
  - Site contacts follow their site.
  - Visits, attachments, submissions and reports follow their project: seeing them uses `can_see_project`, writing them uses `can_work_on_project`.
  - Form templates: admins change them; every member reads published ones.
- `customer_name` also returns sub-company names with their parent's name, for display as "Acme › Acme Retail". Workers read names through it, like partners.
- The leak suite grows: another partner's site, visit, photo, reading and report, and their signed URLs, stay not found. So do a worker's non-crew projects.

## Offline capture and sync
Next.js server components and server actions need the network, so the offline part is a **client island**: a field-capture view within the project page. This is an exception to "server components by default", recorded in ADR-0009.

- **Installable app (PWA):** a web app manifest and a service worker cache the app shell and the field-capture view's code, so the view opens with no signal.
- **Field pack:** while online, the device downloads a pack for each project the technician works on into IndexedDB. The pack holds the project, site details and contacts, open tasks, and the published form template versions. It refreshes on every online visit and shows when it was last updated. A "Make available offline" button on a project forces a download.
- **Outbox:** everything captured offline goes into an IndexedDB outbox first: visits, readings, photos (as blobs) and task status changes.
  - Every item has its client-generated id and a timestamp from the device.
  - The UI reads the outbox, so the technician sees their own unsynced work right away, marked "Not synced yet".
- **Sync:** the device syncs when it comes back online, when the app opens, and on a timer while items are waiting.
  - It doesn't rely on Background Sync, which Safari on iOS doesn't support.
  - Each item goes to an idempotent endpoint that accepts the client id: sending the same item twice records it once.
  - Photos upload through a signed URL first, then their metadata.
- **No data loss:**
  - An item leaves the outbox only after the server confirms it.
  - The app asks for persistent storage (`navigator.storage.persist()`) and warns when the device is low on space.
  - A sync indicator shows how many items are waiting.
- **Conflicts:** offline work only creates new records, and changes task status (*my call*). Visits, photos and readings are append-only, so they never conflict. For a task's status, the most recent change by device time wins, and the task's history keeps both (*my call*). Editing existing visits or readings needs a connection.
- **Rejected items:** the server can refuse an item, for example when access was removed while the technician was offline, or the project was deleted. The item stays on the device, marked "Couldn't sync" with the reason, and the technician can export it (JSON plus photos) so nothing is lost. Admins can be told about it (parking lot).
- **Sessions:** if the login expires while offline, capture keeps working, because the outbox belongs to the user on that device. Sync waits until the user signs in again. Another user signing in on the same device doesn't see or sync someone else's outbox.
- **Testing:**
  - Playwright scenarios go offline with `context.setOffline(true)`, capture, come back online and check that the data arrived exactly once.
  - Unit tests cover the outbox and sync logic: retries, duplicates, refusals.
- **Known risk:** browsers can evict storage, iOS more than others. Installing the app to the home screen and persistent storage reduce the risk, and the warnings make it visible. If this turns out to be insufficient, a native wrapper is the fallback (ADR-0009 alternative).

## File storage
- A new package, `@repo/storage`, offers `createUploadUrl`, `createDownloadUrl` and `delete`.
  - It is backed by Google Cloud Storage: a private bucket, in Terraform under `infra/`.
  - Locally it uses `fake-gcs-server` in Docker next to Postgres.
- The server checks access before issuing short-lived signed URLs. The bucket is never public.
- Uploads go straight from the browser to storage, so large photos never pass through Cloud Run.
- On phones, photos come from `<input type="file" accept="image/*" capture>`. The browser shrinks large photos before uploading (*my call*; limits wait on open question 3). The server checks the type and size of every file.
- The real bucket depends on M11. Everything before deploy works with the local backend.

## Generated reports
Rendered on the server as a PDF from a print layout. The approach (Playwright printing to PDF, or `@react-pdf/renderer`) is chosen in its task and gets its own ADR. The contents and branding wait on open question 1.

## Screens (summary)
- **Customers list:** parents, with their sub-companies indented under them.
- **Customer page:** sub-companies, sites, and the projects of the organization and its sub-companies.
- **Site page:** details, on-site contacts, map link, projects at the site.
- **New/edit project:** customer picker ("Parent › Sub-company"), then a required site picker limited to that organization and its sub-companies.
- **Project page:** a Site card, Crew (admins add workers), and Visits, Photos, Readings and Reports tabs. All of it works at phone width.
- **Field view (offline-capable):** the project's site, tasks and forms. Record a visit, fill readings, take photos, with a sync indicator.
- **Settings → Forms (admins):** build, publish and archive templates.
- **Members:** invite links can create Worker memberships.

## Open questions
1. **Generated report:** contents and branding (logo, sections, signatures). Waiting for an example. Blocks task 065.
2. **Reading forms:** field types beyond `number`, `text`, `yes/no`, `choice`, `date` and `photo`, such as a signature or pass/fail per item. Waiting for one or two real forms. Task 060 can start with the six types above.
3. **Photo and file limits:** a maximum size and count per visit, and how long files are kept, including deleted ones.
4. **Worker rules marked *my call*** in the Roles table: no project creation or settings, no task delete, and admins manage crews.
